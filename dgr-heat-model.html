KBS-3 Multi-Barrier Safety Concept & Thermal Design Constraints

The KBS-3 spent nuclear fuel disposal method (developed by SKB, Sweden and implemented at Posiva's ONKALO repository, Finland) relies on a passive, multi-barrier containment system designed for long-term isolation (>100,000 years):

  • Engineered Copper-Cast Iron Canister: A cast-iron inner insert provides structural mechanical strength against glacial loads, surrounded by a corrosion-resistant copper outer shell (50 mm thick).
  • Compacted Bentonite Buffer: Highly compacted sodium bentonite clay blocks line each vertical deposition hole. Bentonite swells upon water contact, providing low hydraulic conductivity, self-healing against fracturing, and geochemical buffering.
  • Crystalline Rock Matrix: Low-permeability granitic host bedrock at depths of 400 to 500 m provides a stable, reducing hydrogeological environment.

Thermal Engineering Constraint: Radioactive decay heat Q(t) = Q0 e-λt conducts through the buffer into the rock matrix. To prevent mineralogical transformation of montmorillonite in the bentonite buffer, the peak temperature at the canister-buffer interface must remain strictly below 95 °C. Canister and drift spacing (S) is therefore a critical variable to avoid thermal plume interference between adjacent deposition drifts.
